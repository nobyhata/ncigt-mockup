Partner with the center

Talk to us. We can help.

We have been fortunate to work with research groups at many sites through our collaborative and service projects. In this cycle we are especially interested in partnerships that connect with our technology research and development projects. Your work does not need to match a project directly: any part of our methods, tools or technology can be a starting point. Contact the center, or reach out to a project lead directly.

1 Start a conversation Tell us about your research. No formal proposal is needed, and we will connect you with the right investigators.
2 Collaborate Begin working together as a collaborator, using our tools, methods or expertise where they help.
3 Grow into a formal partnership When the work grows, it can become a collaborative or service project with the center.

Formal partnerships

When a collaboration grows, it can become a collaborative or service project, a formal, ongoing relationship with the center. These are open to groups that lead active, funded research, such as an NIH or comparable peer-reviewed award, supporting the proposed work.

Collaborative project

Your research question needs technology that does not yet exist. You work with center investigators, your needs help shape our development, and the relationship brings co-authored publications, shared data, letters of support for your grants and possible clinical studies in AMIGO.

Service project

You use software, methods, equipment or data the center has already developed, with training and support from center staff. Your experience informs future improvements, and your publications and talks are featured on this site.

A place to exchange ideas

We hope to bring our partners together at scientific meetings to share results and interests. Current and prospective partners are all welcome. [Planned: collaborative and service project summit]

Industry partners

From a clinical need to a device ready for patients

We particularly welcome companies. Center investigators have a long record of industry collaboration through NIH SBIR and STTR awards, helping partners define needs with our clinicians, prototype with clinical equipment, test feasibility from phantom to patient, and gather data for regulatory review.
