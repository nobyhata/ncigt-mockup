Training in Image-Guidance, Precision Diagnosis and Therapy

TRAINING IN IMAGE GUIDANCE, PRECISION DIAGNOSIS AND THERAPY T32 PROGRAM

The Training in Image Guidance, Precision Diagnosis and Therapy (IGT) T32 Program at Brigham and Women’s Hospital and Harvard Medical School, led by Dr. Lauren O’Donnell and Dr. Fiona Fennessy, trains outstanding postdoctoral fellows at the intersection of medicine and technology.

Supported by the National Institutes of Health (T32EB025823), the program provides a highly interdisciplinary training environment spanning engineering, computer science, medical imaging, artificial intelligence, multiomics, surgical robotics, targeted therapeutics, and clinical research.

PROGRAM OVERVIEW

Image-guided therapy (IGT) plays a crucial role in modern medicine, providing a precise and personalized roadmap for interventions such as biopsy, ablation, surgery, deep brain stimulation, and radiation oncology. IGT brings together image guidance—including image acquisition, processing, and analysis—and precision diagnosis—including integration of data from multiple sources—to guide therapies across a broad range of clinical disciplines.

Our program prepares the next generation of MD and PhD investigators to advance this rapidly evolving field through integrated clinical and technical training.

The IGT T32 provides a two-year mentored postdoctoral research experience that combines cutting-edge technical research with exposure to clinical image-guided diagnosis and therapy. Each fellow receives complementary technical and clinical mentorship while participating in interdisciplinary research, educational activities, and career development designed to prepare for an independent career in biomedical research.

ELIGIBILITY & FELLOWSHIP SUPPORT

The IGT T32 supports four postdoctoral fellowship positions in a two-year mentored research program.

Applicants must hold an MD, PhD, or equivalent doctoral degree and have a scientific or clinical background relevant to the program. PhD applicants are welcome from a broad range of scientific, engineering, and computational disciplines. MD applicants must have completed residency training and may come from specialties including diagnostic and interventional radiology, surgery, radiation oncology, pathology, and other areas relevant to image-guided therapy.

In accordance with NIH requirements, trainees must be U.S. citizens, noncitizen nationals, or permanent residents at the time of appointment.

Compensation is commensurate with qualifications and years of training, in accordance with BWH postdoctoral guidelines.

HOW TO APPLY

Applications are accepted on a rolling basis. Start dates are flexible depending on available trainee slots.

Applicants should submit the following materials via email:

  • Curriculum vitae (CV)
  • Personal statement describing short- and long-term career goals and how participation in the IGT T32 Program would support those goals
  • Research interests and potential mentor(s) selected from the program’s research themes
  • Names and contact information for three references; letters may be requested during the selection process
  • Confirmation of U.S. citizenship, noncitizen national, or permanent resident status

SELECTION

Applications are reviewed based on academic and research accomplishments, potential for career development, alignment with the program’s research and training themes, and potential to benefit from interdisciplinary clinical and technical mentorship. Selected applicants will be invited to interview.

FACULTY MENTORS & RESEARCH THEMES

Fellows are matched with faculty mentors based on their research interests and career goals, with interdisciplinary mentorship designed to provide complementary technical and clinical perspectives. Faculty span the BWH Departments of Radiology, Neurosurgery, Pathology, Psychiatry, and Neurology, providing opportunities for research across a broad range of clinical disciplines.

1. MULTIOMICS FOR IGT: RADIOMICS, PATHOMICS, METABOLOMICS

Faculty: Fiona Fennessy, MD, PhD | Sabina Signoretti, MD | Alexander Lin, PhD

This theme explores how imaging can be integrated with pathology, genomics, spectroscopy, and metabolomics to better characterize tumor heterogeneity and guide precision diagnosis and treatment. Fellows gain exposure to diagnostic and interventional radiology, pathology, and computational approaches for integrating multiomic data into clinically meaningful models.

2. ADVANCED MR AND COMPUTATIONAL IMAGE SCIENCE

Faculty: Lauren O’Donnell, PhD | Yogesh Rathi, PhD | Yanmei Tie, PhD | Daniel Paech, MD, PhD | Carl-Fredrik Westin, PhD

This theme combines state-of-the-art MRI with advanced computational methods for studying brain structure, function, and disease. Research opportunities include 7T and high-resolution MRI, diffusion and microstructure imaging, MR spectroscopy, brain mapping, deep learning, image-guided interventions, and open-source software development using platforms such as 3D Slicer and SlicerDMRI.

3. MICRODEVICES AND SURGICAL ROBOTICS

Faculty: Oliver Jonas, PhD | Nobuhiko Hata, PhD | Jayender Jagadeesan, PhD | Ron Kikinis, MD | Junichi Tokuda, PhD

This theme focuses on developing novel devices and robotic technologies for image-guided diagnosis and therapy. Projects span drug-releasing intratumoral microdevices, robotic catheters and scopes, shape-sensing needles, and image-guided systems for minimally invasive procedures, with strong opportunities for clinical translation and collaboration with industry.

4. TARGETED BRAIN THERAPEUTICS

Faculty: Nathan McDannold, PhD | Nicholas Todd, PhD | Michael Fox, MD, PhD

This theme brings together imaging, neuroscience, and therapeutic technology to develop and improve treatments that target brain circuits. Fellows can pursue research in MRI-guided focused ultrasound, deep brain stimulation, neuromodulation, and network-based approaches to treating neurological and psychiatric disorders within a highly interdisciplinary clinical and research environment.

5. MACHINE LEARNING FOR ROBUST SENSING AND IMAGING

Faculty: Sarah Frisken, PhD | Alexandra Golby, MD | Bruno Madore, PhD | Tina Kapur, PhD

This theme develops machine-learning methods that combine medical images with real-time imaging and sensor data to improve image-guided procedures. Projects include intraoperative tumor segmentation, image registration for neuronavigation, surgical tool tracking, multimodal imaging, and ML-enhanced image acquisition, with opportunities for both algorithm development and clinical application.

6. CROSS-SCALE BIOMARKER INTEGRATION

Faculty: Nathalie Agar, PhD | Sandro Santagata, MD, PhD

This theme integrates information across molecular, cellular, microscopic, and macroscopic scales to better characterize disease and guide precision treatment. Fellows can work with emerging technologies including mass spectrometry imaging, spatial metabolomics, single-cell RNA sequencing, multiplexed tissue imaging, and computational approaches for relating molecular biomarkers to imaging and clinical information.

TRAINING EXPERIENCE

Each fellow receives interdisciplinary co-mentorship, with a primary mentor overseeing day-to-day research and training and a secondary mentor providing complementary expertise, regular feedback, and a broader clinical or technical perspective.

Training is tailored to each fellow’s research interests and career goals, with fellows identifying thematic areas and mentors during the interview and selection process. The curriculum evolves as new technologies emerge and are translated into clinical practice.

The training experience includes six core components:

  • Interdisciplinary mentorship from complementary technical and clinical faculty
  • A mentored research project aligned with the fellow’s scientific and career goals
  • Hands-on experience in image-guided therapy and exposure to clinical IGT environments
  • Personalized education and career development
  • Scientific communication and grant-writing training
  • Multidisciplinary training and community engagement, including the T32 seminar series and program events
