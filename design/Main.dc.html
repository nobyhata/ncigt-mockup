AMIGO and clinical research

Clinical research in the AMIGO suite and beyond

Clinical investigators across Brigham and Women's Hospital use the center's imaging, guidance and analysis resources in their own clinical studies. Clinicians interested in joining them are warmly invited.

The AMIGO suite

The Advanced Multi-modality Image Guided Operating suite combines MRI, PET/CT and X-ray imaging with a fully equipped procedure room, so imaging, navigation and robotics research can take place during clinical procedures.

Formal partnerships

When a collaboration grows, it can become a collaborative or service project, a formal, ongoing relationship with the center. These are open to groups that lead active, funded research, such as an NIH or comparable peer-reviewed award, supporting the proposed work.

Collaborative project

Your research question needs technology that does not yet exist. You work with center investigators, your needs help shape our development, and the relationship brings co-authored publications, shared data, letters of support for your grants and possible clinical studies in AMIGO.

Service project

You use software, methods, equipment or data the center has already developed, with training and support from center staff. Your experience informs future improvements, and your publications and talks are featured on this site.

A place to exchange ideas

We hope to bring our partners together at scientific meetings to share results and interests. Current and prospective partners are all welcome. [Planned: collaborative and service project summit]

Acknowledging the center

If the center's technology, data, equipment or staff contributed to your work, please include this sentence in your acknowledgments. It helps us show the value of this shared resource and keep it available.

This work was supported in part by the Advanced Technologies - National Center for Image Guided Therapy (AT-NCIGT), funded by the National Institute of Biomedical Imaging and Bioengineering of the National Institutes of Health under award P41EB028741.

History of the center

The center has been supported through several NIH funding cycles, first as the National Center for Image Guided Therapy (NCIGT) and now as AT-NCIGT. We gratefully acknowledge the investigators, staff and trainees of each cycle, whose contributions form the foundation of the center's current work.
