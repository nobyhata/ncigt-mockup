NIH NIBIB P41 National Resource Center · Mass General Brigham · Harvard Medical School

Advanced Technologies – National Center for Image Guided Therapy

A national resource center funded by NIBIB through the P41 program. We create new technology for image-guided therapy, develop it hand in hand with collaborating research and clinical teams, and share it with the research community through training and dissemination.

Research

Three technology research and development projects

Each project takes a clinical problem from new technology to testing in patients. Visit a project to see its results, its team and how to work with it.

TR&D 1

Imaging cancer heterogeneity

Sub-millimeter diffusion MRI and quantitative CEST at 3T and 7T to map tumor structure and chemistry.

Recent result: Optimized diffusion MRI that accounts for signal decay in tissue (Magn Reson Med, 2026).
Explore the project and its latest paper →

Image: Latest TR&D 1 paper: radial diffusivity and fractional anisotropy maps from a high-performance gradient 3T scanner. Zhu et al., Hum Brain Mapp, Nov 2026, Fig. 2 (cropped), CC BY 4.0. Paper

TR&D 2

Implantable microdevices

Microdevices that test many drugs inside a patient’s tumor, with machine learning that links response to imaging.

Recent result: A catheter-based platform for interventional phase-0 drug response (Ann Biomed Eng, 2026).
Explore the project and its latest paper →

Image: Latest TR&D 2 paper: an implantable microdevice releasing labeled cells. Panikkanvalappil et al., Sci Adv, Aug 2026, Fig. 2 (cropped), CC BY-NC 4.0. Paper

TR&D 3

Enabling technologies for data-driven interventions

Robotic needle guidance and augmented-reality navigation for precise procedures across imaging modalities.

Recent result: Initial patient study of a robotic template for MRI-guided prostate biopsy (J Med Robot Res, 2026).
Explore the project and its latest paper →

Image: Recent TR&D 3 paper: predicted needle deflection with confidence bands on segmented prostate MRI. Hoffman et al., Med Phys, Feb 2026, Fig. 7 (cropped), CC BY 4.0. Paper

AMIGO and clinical research

Clinical research in the AMIGO suite and beyond

Clinical investigators across Brigham and Women's Hospital use the center's imaging, guidance and analysis resources in their own clinical studies. Clinicians interested in joining them are warmly invited.

The AMIGO suite

The Advanced Multi-modality Image Guided Operating suite combines MRI, PET/CT and X-ray imaging with a fully equipped procedure room, so imaging, navigation and robotics research can take place during clinical procedures.

Industry partners

From a clinical need to a device ready for patients

We particularly welcome companies. Center investigators have a long record of industry collaboration through NIH SBIR and STTR awards, helping partners define needs with our clinicians, prototype with clinical equipment, test feasibility from phantom to patient, and gather data for regulatory review.

Partner with the center

Talk to us. We can help.

We have been fortunate to work with research groups at many sites through our collaborative and service projects. In this cycle we are especially interested in partnerships that connect with our technology research and development projects. Your work does not need to match a project directly: any part of our methods, tools or technology can be a starting point. Contact the center, or reach out to a project lead directly.

1 Start a conversation Tell us about your research. No formal proposal is needed, and we will connect you with the right investigators.
2 Collaborate Begin working together as a collaborator, using our tools, methods or expertise where they help.
3 Grow into a formal partnership When the work grows, it can become a collaborative or service project with the center.
