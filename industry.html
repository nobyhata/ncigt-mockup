Industry partnerships

From a clinical need to a device ready for patients

We particularly welcome companies developing image-guided devices and software. Center investigators have a long record of working with industry, most often through NIH Small Business Innovation Research (SBIR) and Small Business Technology Transfer (STTR) awards.

Talk to us about your product

How we can help

Support at every stage of development

We work alongside your team and our clinical colleagues at Brigham and Women's Hospital, from understanding the clinical problem to generating the evidence needed for regulatory review.

  1. 1 Define the clinical need Work with our clinicians to write a point-of-view statement of the user, need and insight, without assuming a solution.
  2. 2 Explore solutions Turn the need into "How might we" questions and choose the concepts worth building.
  3. 3 Prototype with clinical equipment Build and test prototypes with clinical scanners, navigation systems and the AMIGO suite.
  4. 4 Test feasibility at every level Phantom, ex vivo and in vivo studies, then human subject studies with BWH clinical investigators.
  5. 5 Prepare for regulatory review Help collecting and organizing bench, preclinical and clinical data for regulatory submissions.

Clinical partners

Access to clinicians in radiology, surgery, urology, neurosurgery and interventional radiology who can define needs, test prototypes and lead clinical studies.

Clinical equipment

Prototype testing with clinical scanners and the AMIGO suite, so feasibility is studied under real procedural conditions.

Grant partnership

Experience as academic partners on SBIR and STTR applications, including study design, letters of support and subaward planning.

Success stories

Industry partners who worked with us

Gregory Fischer, PhD · Worcester Polytechnic Institute and AiM Medical Robotics

From a research collaboration to an MRI-guided neurosurgery robot

Gregory Fischer has worked with center investigators for more than fifteen years on robots that operate inside the MRI scanner. His robots connect to 3D Slicer through OpenIGTLink, the open protocol he co-developed with the center, and he and Nobuhiko Hata led an NIBIB R01 together at the Brigham on robot-assisted prostate interventions.

That work is now moving toward patients through industry. AiM Medical Robotics received an NIH SBIR Phase II award in 2026 to develop an MRI-guided neurosurgery robot for ablation of brain tumors, with the Brigham as its clinical partner.

Partners: Gregory Fischer (WPI, AiM Medical Robotics); Nobuhiko Hata, Junichi Tokuda and colleagues (BWH) · R44CA314728 on NIH RePORTER →

Milestones
2009 OpenIGTLink published with Junichi Tokuda and center colleagues, linking MRI robots to navigation software
2013 NIH R01 at WPI for MRI-guided conformal ablation of brain tumors
2021 NIBIB R01EB030539 with Nobuhiko Hata at BWH: physician-assistance technology for robotic prostate interventions
2026 NIH SBIR Phase II R44CA314728 to AiM Medical Robotics: MRI-guided neurosurgery robot for brain tumor ablation, with BWH
Stratagen Bio · NIH SBIR R43CA261401, R43CA281444

Measuring tumor oxygenation for radiation therapy

With BWH Radiation Oncology, center investigators supplied MRI protocols and analysis for the OxyTrack oxygen sensor, moving from phantom to swine validation toward in vivo measurement. [In progress]

Navigation Sciences, Inc. · NIH STTR R41CA261420, R41CA306611

Finding and removing small lung nodules

Small lung nodules are hard to find during surgery. With center investigator Jagadeesan Jayender as principal investigator, Navigation Sciences received an STTR award in 2021 for a bronchoscopic navigation system to localize and excise nodules, and a second in 2025 that combines targeted localization with optimized surgical resection for lung cancer.

Physical Sciences Inc. · NIH SBIR R44CA224853

A robotic needle-guide template for MRI-guided prostate biopsy

Physical Sciences Inc. and center investigators replaced the fixed grid of a conventional needle-guide template with angulated and motorized templates, and carried the device into patients at the Brigham (J Med Robot Res, 2026).

Other industry collaborations include open-source software work with Kitware and NVIDIA, and navigation development with Brainlab engineers. [verify list]
